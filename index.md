I am pursuing a B.S. in Geological Sciences at the University of Southern California (USC), 
where I have been conducting research since 2023 in Prof. Sam Silva's 
<a href="https://www.earthdatausc.com/">Atmospheric Composition & Earth Data Science</a> lab. 
My research journey began there, where I developed skills in atmospheric data analysis and 
grew increasingly interested in atmospheric sciences. With Sam, I am currently working on a paper for a project studying ozone dry deposition on Saguaro Cacti. My second project focuses on bulk stomatal resistance, where I am conducting a sensitivity analysis of bulk surface resistance parameters to investigate the impact they have on ozone dry deposition predictions.

In the summer of 2025, I participated in the NASA Student Airborne Research Program (SARP), studying methane 
emissions in wetland ecosystems. This experience gave me the opportunity to fly aboard a research 
aircraft and collect airborne measurements firsthand. I also utilized data that I gathered myself in the air and on the field, while also combining remote-sensing and satellite data for my project. 

In the summer of 2026, I interned at NSF NCAR as part of the SOARS (Significant Opportunities in Atmospheric Research and Science) program. Working with the UCP COMET team on their 3D-PAWS (3D-Printed Automatic Weather Station) low-cost, air quality sensors, my project focused on characterizing and correcting biases in PM2.5 air quality measurements for sensors deployed across three climatically distinct sites. This project 
sits at the intersection of sensor science and environmental justice where I intend to improve the reliability 
of low-cost air quality monitoring in across all communities.
