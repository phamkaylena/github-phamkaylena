### Common Threads Between Knitting and Engineering
**Illumin Magazine, USC Viterbi School of Engineering** · Volume XXVI, Issue I · May 2026

About: Published article exploring the overlap between knitting and engineering principles where I cover the binary logic of stitch patterns, the mechanics of how loops give knitted fabric its stretch and structure, and how these principles have influenced real research in soft robotics and biomedical engineering.

Abstract: Knitting is a textile craft that bridges art, engineering, and computation together. From the binary structure of stitch patterns, to the mechanical properties of knitted garments, this craft is rooted in scientific principles that provide garments with different forms and functions. Understanding the science of knitting inspires modern day engineering innovations, showcasing knitting’s interdisciplinary nature that weaves creativity with science.

### Characterizing PM2.5 Data in Low-Cost Air Quality Sensors
**NSF NCAR SOARS** · August 2026

About: Writing deliverable for NSF SOARS 2026 where I wrote a research paper on the work I conducted over the course of my internship.

Abstract: Short and long-term exposure to fine particulate matter, specifically particles with a diameter below 2.5 μm (PM2.5) is attributed to severe human health impacts. Conventional air quality monitoring for PM2.5 is limited on their spatial resolution and costs, often requiring high-cost instrumentation and time-intensive work to record observations. Low-cost air quality sensors offer the ability to measure air pollutants at higher network density compared to conventional methods. 3D-Printed Automatic Weather Station (3D-PAWS) is a low-cost weather and environmental monitoring system that seeks to provide accessible and reliable environmental observations. Given the potentials of 3D-PAWS Air Quality Sensors (3D-PAWS AQ), we find that they have an important role in measuring PM2.5.
 
In this work, we characterize the 3D-PAWS AQ through analyzing its biases and uncertainties to improve its usability. Our study area focuses on Athens, Georgia; Boulder, Colorado; and Lee Park, Virginia, where we evaluate 3D-PAWS data collocated with air quality data sourced from EPA reference-grade air quality monitors (Teledyne 640x) for comparison. We first processed and cleaned our instrument data to smoothen and reduce noise in our dataset. Afterwards, we applied a regression analysis to calibrate the data with respect to the reference data. We find that 3D-PAWS is capable of capturing features that are reflected in reference monitors and a calibration process can be applied to mitigate biases. Our results emphasize the importance in evaluating the accuracy of low-cost sensors to improve PM2.5 monitoring across varied environmental regimes.
